Scholar's Choice · UI/UX Refresh · Phase 1 Facelift

Institution vs Student Native

Three visual directions for the January 2027 facelift, specified side by side. A extends the system already running on the LTC Lou Yang feature. B proposes a high-energy student-facing alternative — lime over deep green, with the trust carried by the ground rather than by restraint. C keeps A's structure and swaps its one weak colour. All three cover the same nineteen Phase 1 elements — only the treatment differs.

What changed to make C a real option

C was drawn from A's original navy-and-gold system, and promoting it required fixing the thing that made gold awkward: gold cannot set text on paper (2.2:1), so it only worked on half the site. C's ember ships as a pair — #E8552F for fills and #B33A18 for text on light — clearing AA in both. A has since moved to graphite and cobalt, so C is no longer a variant of A; it is the one direction still carrying the navy.

Cobalt cannot touch a dark ground

Cobalt against graphite measures 2.23:1 — as text or as a button fill on a dark panel it has no edge at all. Gold escaped this only by being light, which is also why gold could never be a link on paper. So buttons invert: cobalt fill with white text on light (7.94:1), bone fill with cobalt text on dark (7.34:1 text, 16.39:1 edge). Powder and powder-lt carry type on dark.

Lime never sets text on a light ground

Against paper, lime measures 1.26:1 — effectively invisible. It is a fill and a dark-ground colour only, where it reaches 12.99:1. Teal remains the text colour for links and labels on light, which is also what keeps the credibility attached to reading and comparing rather than to the loud furniture.

Ember is a four-value ramp, split by contrast role

Bright ember sits behind no text at all — it is rules and ticks. Ember deep fills buttons under white text and sets links on paper. Ember light takes display type on navy; ember pale takes small text on navy. Four values sounds like a lot until you see what happens with fewer — see the correction note in section 06.

Tinted supports

Family B v3 marks the Phase 2 pathway by inverting the card to the deep-green ground with a lime icon, rather than tinting it. The break in the row's rhythm is the most adventurous single move in the direction, and it earns its place by carrying real information — that card is the one that is not built yet. A and C distinguish the same three cards by the colour of the 3px rule alone.

Two display serifs now share the page

Playfair sets headlines; DM Serif italic still sets standfirsts and pull-quotes. Both are high-contrast display serifs of a similar era, so they read as a near-match rather than a deliberate pairing. Bricolage is playful without being juvenile

Poppins (v1) read as a children's app because it is built on near-perfect circles. Archivo (v2) fixed that but had no personality left. Bricolage Grotesque is deliberately irregular — slightly condensed, with odd characterful curves — so it carries tone the way a magazine masthead does rather than the way a toy does. At 800 with −.045em tracking it is the most distinctive display voice of the three, and still a sans, so it stays clearly apart from A's Playfair and C's Bebas.

The radius nudge is doing real work

06 — Contrast

Measured pairs

Every combination used for text, measured two ways. WCAG 2.1 is the compliance standard — 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 at 18.66px bold or 24px+. APCA Lc is the perceptual measure behind the draft WCAG 3 guidance, and it is the one that predicts what the eye actually does: Lc 75 for body text, Lc 60 for headlines and 16px bold, Lc 45 as the floor for large bold type. Both columns matter, because they disagree — and where they disagree, they disagree about saturated oranges.

Correction — and why the second column is here

An earlier version of this guide put C's button at navy on bright ember #E8552F, and called it a pass on the strength of 4.98:1. It was reported as too dim on sight, and the eye was right: the same pair scores Lc 39.8, under the Lc 45 floor for large bold text and nowhere near the ~60 a 13px uppercase label wants. WCAG 2.x computes luminance in a way that systematically over-rates saturated mid-tone oranges and reds, so a legal ratio can still be an unreadable button. C's buttons are now white on ember-deep — 5.9:1 and Lc 83.9. Two other pairs failed the same way and were fixed: ember-light was carrying 10–13px text on navy at Lc 47.5, now ember-pale at Lc 68.7.

Family B had the same fault, and two reworks removed it. v1's ink-on-coral was 5.78:1 at Lc 45.9 — legal, but bottom of the perceptual range for a bold label. v3's deep-green-on-lime is 12.99:1 at Lc 86.3, the strongest primary button anywhere in this project. The one rule lime brings with it is the inverse of A's gold rule: it may never set text on a light ground.

The standing rules: A — cobalt is a light-ground colour only; powder carries type on dark. B — lime is a fill and dark-ground colour only; teal carries text on light. C — bright ember touches no text at all.

07 — Decision

Which one, and when

All three meet the brief. The choice is about which risk the business would rather carry into January 2027.

Pick A if

Weighting precision and neutrality

  • Sales need a credible, premium masthead story for agency conversations
  • Agency crests and uniform-group creative must sit natively in the page
  • The January date is at risk and you want zero new components
  • A cool, neutral shell is wanted so sponsor creative supplies the colour
  • You would rather look precise than warm

Pick B if

Weighting the 17–21 audience

  • Pathway-module engagement is the metric that matters most
  • The site should stop a scrolling seventeen-year-old, not just reassure a parent
  • There is appetite to rebuild the component set rather than restyle it
  • Sales are comfortable placing agency crests beside a high-energy palette
  • You would rather be memorable than safe

Pick C if

Weighting both, on A's budget

  • The relaunch needs to look visibly new, not like a re-skin of today
  • Sponsors still need an institutional frame around their creative
  • You want one action colour that works everywhere, without exceptions
  • Engineering time is the binding constraint, not design ambition
  • You would rather move one step than two

How to settle it

These are now three genuinely separate directions, which was not true earlier in the process. C began as A with one token changed; A has since moved to graphite, cobalt and Playfair while C kept navy, ember and Bebas, so no two of them share a ground, an accent or a display face. That is worth saying out loud in the review, because the shortcut of "A and C are basically the same, so really it is A-or-B" no longer holds. Compare all three on their own terms.
