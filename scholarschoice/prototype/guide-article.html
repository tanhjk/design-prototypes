How long should a scholarship essay be? Word counts for Singapore applications – NUS, NTU, government and more

From 50-word responses to longer personal essays, application limits can vary widely. Here’s what some common Singapore scholarships ask for.

By Scholar's Choice team

Published 2 Oct 2026

Share

How long should a scholarship essay be? There is no single answer.

Some applications ask for a personal essay of a few hundred words. Others give you several short questions with strict word or character limits. And some scholarship providers do not publish their requirements until applications open.

The most important rule is simple: follow the limit given. If you are preparing your application early and do not have one yet, this guide gives you an idea of what some government and university scholarships in Singapore ask for.

Word count for Singapore government scholarship essays

There is no standard word count for government scholarship essays in Singapore, and some providers, such as the Public Service Commission, do not publish their essay requirements online. Requirements can also change between application cycles.

However, some scholarships, like the Singapore-Industry Scholarship (SgIS), do. SgIS applicants are asked to write an essay of up to 500 words about themselves and their career aspirations to support their submission.

If applications have not opened yet

Focus on identifying the experiences and examples you want to write about rather than hitting a particular word count. Once applications open, check the official portal before you start drafting.

Word count for Singapore university scholarship personal statements or essays

Likewise, scholarship essays for universities – often referred to as personal statements – differ in their length requirements and formats.

National University of Singapore (NUS) scholarships

When applying for NUS scholarships, you would need to answer five short questions on why you deserve the scholarship, and how you have exemplified the NUS values of Innovation, Resilience, Excellence, Respect and Integrity. Each response has character limits. You only need to submit one set of written responses.

Mid-term scholarships

Mid-term scholarships may have additional essay requirements, so keep a lookout for what is needed through the official portals.

Nanyang Technological University (NTU) scholarships

Applying for an NTU scholarship? You will typically need to write a personal essay of no more than 250 words. Only one essay is required even if you selected multiple scholarships in your application.

Singapore Management University (SMU) scholarships

SMU scholarships applied for together with admission, such as the SMU Global Impact Scholarship and the Lee Kong Chian Scholars’ Programme, require a personal essay of no more than 300 words.

Additional questions

You may also need to answer additional questions in 50 words or less. Short answers are not necessarily easier – with 50 words or less, every example needs to earn its place.

Singapore University of Technology and Design (SUTD) scholarships

If you apply for undergraduate programmes at SUTD, you can indicate your interest in scholarships. This will prompt a set of personal insight questions you will need to answer. This is where you can present additional information to help your application. The character limits will be visible within the portal.

Singapore Institute of Technology (SIT) scholarships

SIT scholarship applications require a personal statement of no more than 500 words at the point of admission. Only one essay is required.

Singapore University of Social Sciences (SUSS) scholarships

While the university does not state the need for an essay in its scholarship application, it is good to note that all undergraduate applicants will need to submit a 500-word reflection essay regardless.
