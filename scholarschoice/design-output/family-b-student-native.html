Family B
Student Native

Version 3 — lime as the key colour, Bricolage Grotesque carrying the tone, and the trust held by the deep green underneath rather than by restraint.

V2 solved "childish" by going quiet, and went too far — safe reads as forgettable, and a seventeen-year-old scrolling past has no reason to stop. V3 keeps the same warm-neutral base and the same teal, but puts a high-energy lime in the lead and swaps the neutral grotesque for a display face with real personality. The seriousness now comes from the near-black green ground and the editorial structure, not from the absence of anything interesting.

Scope: all nineteen Phase 1 items from the UI/UX Refresh PRD. Phase 2 items appear as reserved placement only.

Where the adventure comes from

01 · Bricolage Grotesque at 700/800 with −.04em tracking. It is a deliberately irregular grotesque — slightly condensed, with odd, characterful curves that Inter and Poppins both lack. It is playful in the way a good magazine is playful, not in the way a children's app is. Crucially it is still a sans, so it stays clearly apart from A's Playfair and C's Bebas.
02 · Lime leads, green grounds it. Lime #C8F04A is the key: buttons, active nav, the hero slab, tags, the play button, the CTA band. It sits on a near-black green #0F1F1C rather than a neutral black, so the whole palette reads as one family instead of "a bright colour on grey".
03 · An inverted card in the pathway row. The third card flips to the dark ground with a lime icon. It breaks the grid's rhythm deliberately — and it does useful work, because that card is the Phase 2 placement.
04 · Full-bleed lime CTA band. A whole section in the key colour, with the button inverted to deep-on-lime. This is the page's loudest moment and it is placed where a decision is being asked for.
05 · Sharper geometry. Radius down from 6px to 4px, tags to 3px. Combined with heavier display type, sharp corners read editorial and confident rather than app-like.

Where the trust is held

Teal is still doing the credibility work — it sets every link on a light ground and the secondary button, so the calm colour is the one attached to reading and comparing. Lime never sets text on a light ground: at 1.26:1 against paper it is illegible, so it is confined to fills and to dark grounds where it measures 12.99:1. Body copy stays Inter at 1.02rem/1.74, the article structure is unchanged, and the warm paper keeps the reading surface quiet. The energy is all in the furniture; none of it is in the running text.

SAF Merit Scholarship · RSAF · 2026

Pilot, mother, mentor — and still the one flying the aircraft

Standfirst — own section, excluded from H2/H3 How LTC Lou Yang commands naval helicopters off moving frigates, raises two young sons, and returns to the flying club that started it all.
ScholarLTC Lou Yang, 37
AwardSAF Merit
Read atDuke & Yale
Topics MINDEFRSAFOverseasEngineeringBondedUniformed servicesJC route

1 The story

How the decision actually got made

A Singapore Youth Flying Club talk at her junior college changed everything. There was no grand plan — just a chance session, a first flight, and what she calls falling in love with flying.

The scholarship came after, not before. By the time the SAF Merit offer arrived, the question was no longer whether to serve but where the degrees would take her.

What the family said

Her parents were split, which turned out to be useful. Arguing both sides out loud made the trade-offs concrete in a way a comparison table never had.

The SAF difference: careers are planned with life stages in mind. The Servicewomen Network actively surfaces issues unique to women in uniform — and gets results.

"At work, I give my best, so that when I return to my children, I can be fully present with them."
LTC Lou Yang · SAF Merit Scholarship recipient

2 The work

What the job turned out to be

Deployments last a week or two, sometimes a month. At sea she manages complex multi-crew operations, coordinating navy deck crews and air force personnel in real time — landing a 20-metre Seahawk on a deck that will not stay still.

Between postings there has been plans work, flight leadership, a diplomatic aide role between air forces, and media lead on the Shangri-La Dialogue. The engineering degree earns its keep in places the brochure never mentioned.

3 What surprised me

Three things nobody mentions

The cohort matters more than the course. The bond gets shorter psychologically once it actually starts. And returning to the flying club as the pilot rather than the student is stranger, and better, than she expected.

Guides & Tips · Money

How to read a scholarship bond before you sign it

Standfirst — own section, not an H2 Six years, four years, "service obligation" — the wording varies, and so does what it actually costs you. A plain-English walkthrough of the five clauses that matter.

By Editorial · Updated 12 January 2027 · 8 min read

SEO structure fix — the standfirst sits above and outside the heading tree. Body uses a single H1, then sequential H2 › H3. No decorative headings.

Almost every bonded scholarship in Singapore uses one of three contract shapes. Once you can tell them apart, the comparison table stops being a wall of numbers.

1. Bond length is not the same as bond start

Two awards both listed as "six years" can differ by two full years depending on when the clock starts — graduation, commissioning, or completion of initial training.

What to look for in the letter

The phrase "commencing upon" does almost all the work here. Find it before you compare anything else.

2. Coverage is a list, and lists have gaps

  • Tuition — almost always covered in full
  • Maintenance allowance — varies widely by study location
  • Airfare — often one return trip per academic year, not per term
  • Settling-in and computer grants — one-off, easy to miss

3. Breaking a bond has a formula

Liquidated damages are usually pro-rated against served time. Ask for the formula, not the headline figure.
